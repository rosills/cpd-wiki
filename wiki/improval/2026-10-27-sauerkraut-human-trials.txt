Title: Sauerkraut in humans - IBS pilot trial and gut microbiota crossover trial
Date: 27 October 2026
Activity type: Self-directed learning (journal articles)
Time spent: 1 hour (to confirm)

What I learnt:
Following a laboratory study of sauerkraut and the gut barrier, I read two human trials. In a pilot randomised trial of 34 people with IBS (Nielsen, Food and Function 2018), symptom scores improved over six weeks with both pasteurised and live sauerkraut, with no difference between them, but there was no control group without sauerkraut. In a crossover trial of 87 healthy adults (Schropp, Microbiome 2025), 100 g a day for four weeks did not change overall gut microbiome diversity; a few species changed and only pasteurised sauerkraut raised blood short-chain fatty acids. The benefits do not seem to depend on live bacteria, but the evidence remains limited and no human study has measured gut barrier function. [Richard to add or edit.]
