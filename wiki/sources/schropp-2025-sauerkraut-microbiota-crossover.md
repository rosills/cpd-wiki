# Schropp et al. (2025) — sauerkraut and the gut microbiota in healthy adults

Schropp N, Bauer A, Stanislas V, Huang KD, Lesker TR, Bielecka AA, Strowig T, Michels KB. *The impact of regular sauerkraut consumption on the human gut microbiota: a crossover intervention trial.* Microbiome 2025;13:52. Open access. University of Freiburg; registered DRKS00027007. Read 27 October 2026.

In healthy adults, a month of daily sauerkraut, live or pasteurised, did not change overall gut-microbiome diversity. A handful of species changed, and only the pasteurised product raised serum short-chain fatty acids. The authors conclude the healthy microbiome is fairly resilient to short dietary interventions. Relevant to [fermented foods and the intestinal barrier](../topics/fermented-foods-and-gut-barrier.md).

## Design

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Single-centre randomised crossover (fresh-first vs pasteurised-first); blinding not described; **no non-sauerkraut control arm** (washout periods act as baselines) |
| Participants | 87 healthy adults analysed (49 women, 38 men), age 21–69, BMI 18–31; volunteers interested in healthy eating |
| Dose and timing | 100 g/day for 4 weeks per product; 4-week washout before each phase; follow-up 8 weeks after the second phase |
| Products | Fresh: starter-culture ferment dominated by *Lacticaseibacillus paracasei*, pH 3.4. Pasteurised: different, spontaneously fermented product with juniper, 75 °C for 5 min, pH 3.8, no live bacteria |
| Outcomes | Stool shotgun metagenomics and functional profiling; faecal and serum SCFAs; Bristol stool form, frequency, faecal pH |

## Findings

- No significant change in α- or β-diversity after either product.
- Fresh sauerkraut: *L. paracasei* increased (i.e. the starter organism was detectable in stool); faecal butyrate fell.
- Pasteurised sauerkraut: *Anaerostipes hadrus* (a butyrate producer) rose and *Blautia obeum* fell; serum acetate, propionate and butyrate rose.
- Stool form, frequency and faecal pH unchanged; acceptability high (95% fresh, 90% pasteurised).

## Appraisal (agent synthesis)

- **The two products differed in more than pasteurisation** (manufacturer, starter vs spontaneous fermentation, juniper, pH), so "pasteurised vs fresh" contrasts are confounded.
- Healthy volunteers with good baseline diets — limited applicability to IBS or other patients; no symptom or barrier-function outcomes.
- Consumption at home was not supervised; stool samples self-collected.
- The pasteurised product's SCFA signal fits the idea that fermentation metabolites and fibre, not only live organisms, drive effects ([Wei & Marco 2025](wei-marco-2025-fermented-cabbage-metabolome.md); [Nielsen 2018](nielsen-2018-sauerkraut-ibs-pilot.md)) — but this is one finding among many tests.
