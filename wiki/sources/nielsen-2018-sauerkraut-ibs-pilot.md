# Nielsen et al. (2018) — sauerkraut in IBS, pasteurised vs unpasteurised

Sandberg Nielsen E, Garnås E, Juul Jensen K, Hansen LH, Olsen PS, Ritz C, Krych L, Nielsen DS. *Lacto-fermented sauerkraut improves symptoms in IBS patients independent of product pasteurisation – a pilot study.* Food & Function 2018;9:5323–35. **Read from the abstract only** (full text not accessible on 27 October 2026).

A Norwegian pilot trial comparing pasteurised with unpasteurised (live) sauerkraut added to the usual diet of people with IBS. Symptom scores fell in both arms by similar amounts, and the authors suggest the benefit may come more from the food's non-living components (they use the term "potential prebiotics") than from live lactic acid bacteria. Relevant to [fermented foods and the intestinal barrier](../topics/fermented-foods-and-gut-barrier.md).

## Design and results

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Randomised, double-blind pilot; two active arms, **no non-sauerkraut control** |
| Participants | 34 adults with IBS — pasteurised 15, unpasteurised 19 |
| Intervention | Daily sauerkraut added to usual diet for 6 weeks; follow-up at week 8 (daily amount not given in the abstract) |
| Primary outcome | IBS Symptom Severity Score (IBS-SSS) at 0, 2, 4, 6 and 8 weeks |
| IBS-SSS change | Pasteurised −38.6 ± 17.1; unpasteurised −57.0 ± 16.9; both significant within-group (P < 0.04), no significant between-group difference |
| Microbiota (16S, baseline vs week 6) | Composition shifted in both arms; sauerkraut LAB (*L. plantarum*, *L. brevis*) detected more often in the live arm |

## Appraisal (agent synthesis)

- **No placebo or usual-diet control**, so within-group improvement can't be separated from placebo response and regression to the mean — both large in IBS trials. The commonly used threshold for a clinically meaningful IBS-SSS change is about 50 points; only the live arm's mean reached it.
- Authors describe it as underpowered; the "no difference" between arms is therefore not evidence of equivalence.
- Consistent with the in vitro finding that fermentation metabolites, not just live bacteria, may matter ([Wei & Marco 2025](wei-marco-2025-fermented-cabbage-metabolome.md)) — hypothesis-generating only.
